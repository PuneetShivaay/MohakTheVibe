Artificial jewellery does not need special treatment, but it does need a few good habits. Most of what makes a piece look tired is avoidable, and almost all of it comes down to keeping the piece dry and keeping it away from products.

## Why does artificial jewellery lose its shine?

Most artificial jewellery is a base metal with a thin finish on top. That finish is what gives it its colour and shine, and it wears down gradually. Moisture, sweat, perfume, lotion and everyday friction are the usual causes. The less often a piece meets them, the longer it keeps looking the way it did on the day it arrived.

The one rule worth remembering: **last on, first off.** Put your jewellery on after everything else, and take it off before anything else.

## Eight habits that make the difference

1. **Put it on last.** Perfume, hairspray, body lotion and makeup all go on first. Let them dry before you put on your earrings or pendant.
2. **Take it off before water.** That means showers, swimming, washing up and the gym. Water and sweat are the biggest enemies of any finish.
3. **Wipe it after each wear.** A soft, dry cloth takes sweat and oils off in seconds. This one habit does more than any other.
4. **Store it dry.** A bathroom is the worst place for jewellery. Choose a drawer or box in a dry room.
5. **Store it separately.** Give each pair or piece its own pouch or compartment so stones, enamel and hooks do not scratch each other.
6. **Add a silica gel packet.** The small sachets that come in shoe boxes soak up moisture. Drop one in your jewellery box.
7. **Skip harsh cleaners.** Toothpaste, polish dips and chemical cleaners can strip a finish, loosen stones and damage enamel. A soft dry cloth is enough. For stubborn marks, a barely damp cloth, dried straight away.
8. **Handle it gently.** Fasten chains and clasps with care, do not tug a bracelet over your hand, and take earrings off before you sleep.

## Extra care for enamel and stone pieces

Pieces with enamel colour, like Minakari, and pieces with set stones, like Kundan, are more sensitive than plain metal. Water can get behind stones and under enamel, and a hard knock can chip or loosen them. Keep these pieces especially dry, and store them where nothing can press or rub against them. If you are choosing between styles, our guide to [Minakari and Kundan](/journal/minakari-vs-kundan-how-to-tell-the-difference) explains what to look for in each.

## How to store each type

- **Earrings:** in pairs, in a pouch or a box with slots, never loose together
- **Pendants and chains:** flat, or hanging, with the clasp fastened so the chain does not tangle
- **Bracelets:** laid flat or in a roll, so they keep their shape

## Choosing pieces that are easy to look after

Simple shapes are the easiest to keep looking good: studs, small hoops and clean charms have fewer places for moisture to hide. Statement pieces are worth it too, and a few minutes of care after wearing keeps them that way.
