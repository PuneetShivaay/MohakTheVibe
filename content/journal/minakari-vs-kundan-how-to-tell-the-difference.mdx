Minakari and Kundan turn up on almost every Indian earring listing, and the two words are often used as if they mean the same thing. They do not. One is about colour, the other is about stones, and once you know what to look for you can tell them apart in a second.

## What is Minakari?

Minakari, also spelled meenakari, is the art of decorating a metal surface with colour. Enamel is applied to the metal and fired, so it sets into a hard, glossy finish. Jaipur is one of the best-known centres of the craft.

What to look for:

- Bright, saturated colour: reds, greens, blues, pinks and yellows
- Small painted sections separated by fine metal lines
- Floral motifs such as lotus petals, leaves and vines
- A glossy, smooth surface rather than a faceted one

The colour is the star. A Minakari [jhumka](/collections/jhumka) or chandbali carries colour across a room.

## What is Kundan?

Kundan is a setting technique rather than a colour technique. In traditional Kundan jewellery, stones are set into a thin layer of pure gold foil, which gives the piece a soft, luminous glow. It is closely linked with the royal courts of India and with bridal jewellery.

What to look for:

- Flat-backed, polished stones set closely together in a gold-coloured frame
- A glow that comes from the stones and the setting, not from added colour
- Teardrop, floral and peacock shapes
- Weight and presence, often in larger drops and chandbalis

The sparkle is the star. A [Kundan](/collections/kundan) pair brings the feel of a festive evening without needing anything else.

## A quick way to tell

Ask what your eye goes to first. If it is the colour, it is Minakari. If it is the stones, it is Kundan. Many pieces combine the two, with enamel petals around a stone at the centre, so do not be surprised when one pair is a little of both.

## When to wear which

**Minakari** is made for daytime festivals, family gatherings and anything where you want colour. It looks best against plain, softer backgrounds: white, cream, pastel or a single solid colour. Let the earrings be the only loud thing.

**Kundan** suits evenings, weddings and celebrations. It works well with rich solid colours and silk, and it holds its own with a heavier outfit. Because it is already so full of detail, keep the rest of your jewellery quiet.

If you are buying one pair to wear many ways, pick the one that suits the colours you already wear most.

## Caring for both

Enamel and set stones both dislike water and hard knocks. Wipe each pair with a soft dry cloth after wearing, and store them separately in a pouch or box so they do not scratch one another.
